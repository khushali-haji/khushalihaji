Hi, I'm an Urban Designer Researcher Journalist (AI native)UX Designer

Ecotone Edge Effect: the ecological rule where the boundary zone between two habitats, like land and water, supports a much higher density of life because it combines resources from both environments, proving that the most resilient and innovative outcomes happen at the intersection of different worlds.

Reading fiction was my first love. Studying urban design in undergrad changed how I read the stories around me. I saw how economics governed the social fabric of our lives, and how identity, power, and politics were intertwined. I also saw the gap between what gets designed on paper and what actually gets built once corruption and competing interests enter the picture.

That led me to a fellowship at the World Resources Institute, where I researched water and sanitation access for women in low-income Muslim neighborhoods in Ahmedabad, co-producing a website to show our findings. From there I moved through editorial fact-checking at City Observer and scriptwriting with a theater collective. Despite the change in medium, the research and writing problem stayed the same. I wanted a bigger platform.

I came to New York City to study journalism at the Newmark Graduate School at CUNY, specializing in international reporting and documentary filmmaking. I covered immigrant communities across New York, including Bangladeshi, Tibetan, Nepali, Afghan, and Indian diasporas, reporting on asylum policy, caste, domestic violence, and cultural displacement for Documented, Brooklyn Paper, and Central Desi. My short documentary about immigrant women artists received a Special Jury Mention at the 2025 Woodstock Film Festival. I later reported from Liberia with New Narratives, co-reporting two first-reported investigations into carbon credit markets and foreign business influence, using my outsider identity to build trust with sources who were otherwise hard to reach.

Wanting to solve, not just document, I went back to my design roots. As Research and Engagement Lead with Urbanizer in Varanasi, I conducted 40+ interviews with government officials and residents for an infrastructure initiative serving 140 million annual visitors. I managed a small intern team and co-authored the public report and video that helped support a successful second round of funding.

At On a Map, I moved further into information and product design. I designed the information architecture for a live, public-facing conflict-monitoring platform, structured 50+ conflict datasets, and built editorial and visual systems that turned raw geospatial data into something a general reader could actually follow.

At Seat at the Table, I worked across brand, content, and product. I built the documentary's visual identity and designed content across web, social, and print, creating a path from discovery to registration across 15 screenings.

Now, as an AI Native Fellow at Pursuit, I research, wireframe, prototype, and ship. I built Conflict Lens, an AI verification tool that maps conflict actors and cross-references sources in a researcher dashboard. I also designed interactive maps and visualizations from 50,000+ survey responses spanning 15 years, turning hardship data into a case for a financial literacy product for underserved households.

While AI has made building cheaper, it has not made judgment cheaper. Someone still has to decide which question is worth asking, whose problem is being solved, and how to describe a messy human situation precisely enough that a model produces something useful instead of merely plausible. That has been my work for years: moving between research, data, storytelling, and product design, sitting with people to understand what's said and what's left unsaid, then turning that into something a team can build. That is the kind of work I want to keep doing, especially where technology meets public life.
