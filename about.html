Urban Designer→ Journalist→ UX & Product Designer hover

Ecotone Edge Effect: the boundary between two habitats supports more species than either habitat alone. People between worlds are no different.

While reading fiction was my first love, studying urban design in undergrad changed how I read the stories around me. I saw how economics governed the social fabric of our lives, and how identity, power, and politics were intertwined. I also saw the gap between what gets designed on paper and what actually gets built once corruption and competing interests enter the picture. That led me to a fellowship at the World Resources Institute, researching water and sanitation access for women in low-income Muslim neighborhoods in Ahmedabad. From there I moved through editorial fact-checking at a local magazine and scriptwriting with a theater collective. Despite the change in medium, the need to research and build a public-facing narrative to convey it stayed the same.

Seeking a bigger platform, I came to New York City to study journalism at the Newmark Graduate School at CUNY. I specialized in international reporting and documentary filmmaking, covering immigrant communities across the city. I reported on asylum policy, caste, domestic violence, and cultural displacement for Documented, Brooklyn Paper, and Central Desi. My short documentary about immigrant women artists received a Special Jury Mention at the 2025 Woodstock Film Festival. I later reported from Liberia with New Narratives, co-reporting two first-reported investigations into carbon credit markets and foreign business influence, using my outsider identity to build trust with sources who were otherwise hard to reach.

I wanted now to solve, and not just document. I went back to my design roots. As Research and Engagement Lead with Urbanizer in Varanasi, I conducted 40+ interviews with government officials and residents for an infrastructure initiative serving 140 million annual visitors, and co-authored the public report and video that helped support a second round of funding. At On a Map, I designed the information architecture for a live, public-facing conflict-monitoring platform and structured 50+ conflict datasets. At Seat at the Table, I built the documentary campaign's visual identity and designed content across web, social, and print, creating a path from discovery to registration across 15 screenings.

Now, as an AI Native Fellow at Pursuit, I research, wireframe, prototype, and ship. I built Conflict Lens, an AI verification tool that maps conflicts and cross-references sources in a researcher dashboard; interactive maps and visualizations from 50,000+ survey responses for a financial literacy product; and a multilingual chat assistant that helps lower-income women in India market homemade food in their own language with designed marketing assets. My capstone project, SlideVault, a search platform for M Moser Associates, helps the proposal team surface slides from a 45-year archive and suggests outlines for assembling new decks.

While AI has made building cheaper, it has not made judgment cheaper. Someone still has to decide which question is worth asking, whose problem is being solved, which design fits the context, and how to describe a messy, "wicked" situation precisely enough that a model produces something useful. That is where my work lies. I move between research, data, storytelling, and product design, sit with people to understand what's said and what's left unsaid, and turn that into something tangible and buildable, especially for emerging markets and where technology meets public life.
